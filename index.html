About this practice exam

This is one of five full-length practice exams for the ISTQB® Certified Tester Foundation Level (CTFL®) v4.0 exam. Each exam has 40 original questions. The chapter distribution (8-6-4-11-9-2) and the cognitive levels (K1 8, K2 24, K3 8) follow the ISTQB® Exam Structure Tables v1.18.

Every question comes with an option-by-option explanation and a link to the matching section of the free ISTQB® CTFL v4.0 Exam Guide.

Before you book. Exam conditions can change. Check the current rules on the official ISTQB® CTFL page, and with your national Member Board or exam provider, before you sit the exam.

Your data. Your answers and scores are saved only in this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

The four trap types

Every question carries a trap label: the name of the mistake the question is designed to catch. The label appears only in the explanation, after you answer, so that a timed attempt stays a fair measurement.

Source gap. The question tests whether you know a specific list, definition, or fact from the syllabus. If you miss it, the cure is to go back and learn that piece of the source.

Near-miss confusion. The question places two neighbouring concepts side by side, for example confirmation testing and regression testing, or error, defect, and failure. The wrong option is not absurd; it is the concept next door.

Procedure error. The question gives you data and asks you to apply a technique. Each wrong option is the result of a specific mistake in the procedure, and the explanation names the step that went wrong.

Careless reading. The question contains a direction word such as NOT, MOST, or FIRST, or a detail in the scenario that changes the answer.

The companion exam guide uses a second set of labels with a different job. Its labels (sibling concept, right idea in the wrong place, overstated or reversed claim, calculation slip) describe a single wrong option, while the four trap types above describe the question as a whole. The two fit together: a near-miss question, for example, is built around a sibling-concept distractor.
